## Question

For

$$
f(x)=3x^5-2x^3+7x-4,
$$

find $f'(x)$. Then compute $f'(0)$ and $f'(1)$, determine their signs,
and explain what they say about the slopes of the tangent lines at these
points.

## Solution

Apply the power rule $\frac{d}{dx}x^n=nx^{n-1}$ to each power and use
linearity of differentiation. The constant term has derivative zero:

$$
\begin{aligned}
f'(x)
&=3\cdot 5x^{5-1}-2\cdot 3x^{3-1}+7\cdot 1-0\\
&=15x^4-6x^2+7.
\end{aligned}
$$

At $x=0$,

$$
f'(0)=15\cdot 0^4-6\cdot 0^2+7=7>0.
$$

At $x=1$,

$$
f'(1)=15-6+7=16>0.
$$

The derivative at a point is the slope of the tangent line there. Both
values are positive, so the graph is rising at both $x=0$ and $x=1$.
Because $16>7$, the tangent line at $x=1$ is steeper than the tangent line
at $x=0$.
